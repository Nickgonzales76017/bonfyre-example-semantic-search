DNS (Domain Name System) translates human-readable domain names like example.com into IP addresses. The resolution process starts with the recursive resolver (usually your ISP's), which queries root nameservers, then TLD nameservers (.com, .org), and finally authoritative nameservers for the specific domain. Common record types include A (IPv4 address), AAAA (IPv6), CNAME (alias to another domain), MX (mail server), TXT (arbitrary text, used for SPF/DKIM/DMARC email authentication), and NS (nameserver delegation). TTL (Time To Live) controls how long records are cached — lower TTL means faster propagation of changes but more DNS traffic. DNS over HTTPS (DoH) and DNS over TLS (DoT) encrypt queries to prevent eavesdropping. DNSSEC adds cryptographic signatures to prevent spoofing. CDNs use GeoDNS to route users to the nearest edge server.
